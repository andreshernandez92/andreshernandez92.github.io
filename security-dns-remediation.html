The problem

A cyber insurance security scan reported multiple findings on the company's main domain. Over the years, records had been added across many providers, and nobody had a full list of what was exposed to the internet.

Objective: Know every asset under the domain, fix the findings at their root cause, and remove what should not exist anymore.

Step by step

1

Audit the whole domain

  • Enumerated DNS records and traced each to its provider: website builder, a legacy hosting server, AWS load balancers, a VDI provider, a CDN-fronted service desk and Microsoft 365.
  • Found sprawl across six providers and an orphaned legacy mail server still published in DNS.
# Quick checks used during the audit dig +short example.com ANY dig +short TXT example.com # SPF and verification records dig +short TXT _dmarc.example.com # DMARC policy curl -sI http://portal.example.com | grep -i -E "location|set-cookie"
2

Trace findings to root causes

  • Grouped the findings and showed that most came from two sources: the website builder's edge behavior and a vendor-hosted portal's web server configuration.
  • Opened and followed up support tickets with both vendors.

Lesson: group findings before fixing them

Twenty findings can have two causes. Fixing the causes is faster and stops the findings coming back.

3

Fix what is in our control

  • Specified an HTTP-to-HTTPS redirect on the AWS Application Load Balancer and the Secure flag on session cookies for the customer portal.
  • Submitted removal of the orphaned legacy server from DNS and confirmed it drops out of future scans.
  • Prepared SPF and DMARC changes in coordination with the email security gateway.
4

Update policies and governance

  • Published an updated external privacy notice (version 2.2).
  • Took ownership of the corporate device management program for a bring-your-own-device workforce, with HIPAA and SOC 2 as the drivers.
  • Set up compliance ticketing in Freshservice: email to a compliance mailbox creates a ticket, and users were promoted to agents so items can be assigned to them.

Outcome

  • A complete inventory of internet-facing assets across six providers.
  • Most scan findings traced to two root causes with vendor tickets in progress.
  • Defined HTTPS and cookie hardening for the customer portal.
  • An orphaned server removed and email authentication hardening queued.
  • Compliance work moved into a ticketing system with clear owners.
